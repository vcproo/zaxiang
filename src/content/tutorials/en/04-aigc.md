## What these letters mean

<p>AIGC is short for AI Generated Content. Turned into plain words, it means "content generated by AI."</p>

<p>It does not name one piece of software. It is a name for a whole category. "Home appliances" does not mean one refrigerator. It covers the refrigerator, the washing machine, and the TV. Any text, picture, sound, or video made by AI counts as AIGC.</p>

<p>The counterparts are UGC (content ordinary users make themselves) and PGC (content professional teams make). Things online used to come from one of two places: a person filmed it, or a company produced it. Now there is a third source: <strong>a machine made it</strong>.</p>

<p>Besides making things from scratch, it is also very good at changing things: restoring and colorizing an old photo, turning a horizontal video into a vertical one, adding subtitles, taking a spoken Chinese clip and turning it into English with the mouth matched. These used to need a professional. Now an ordinary person can get something close in a few minutes.</p>

## Why can it "draw" at all?

<p>You do not need the technical details. A rough picture is enough.</p>

<p>During training, AI sees a huge number of pictures paired with the words that describe them. Bit by bit it learns roughly what an orange cat looks like, what color a sunset is, and what watercolor brushwork looks like. You give it one sentence, and it follows the patterns it learned, "assembling" a new picture piece by piece.</p>

<p>So what it draws is not cut out of some existing picture. It is newly drawn. But what it can draw depends on what it has seen. That is also the biggest argument around AIGC: for most of the pictures and articles used in training, the original authors never agreed.</p>

## How to tell when AI made it

<p>It is getting harder and harder to tell. A few common tells are still around:</p>

<div class="figure quiz">
<div class="demo-title">Common tells. Click to look</div>
<div class="q">Pictures: hands and text<button class="btn">Look</button><div class="ans">The finger count is wrong, and the joints look awkward. Words on signs, and on the pages of books, turn into gibberish. This has improved a lot lately, but it is still common.</div></div>
<div class="q">Pictures: too perfect<button class="btn">Look</button><div class="ans">The skin is smooth the way a beauty filter makes it, the light is as perfect as a studio, and things in the background smear together if you look closely.</div></div>
<div class="q">Video: the physics is off<button class="btn">Look</button><div class="ans">Feet slide while a person walks, water in a cup does not slosh along, and objects appear or vanish out of nowhere.</div></div>
<div class="q">Text: a lot of words, nothing said<button class="btn">Look</button><div class="ans">Every sentence is smooth, but there are no specific people, events, or numbers. It likes "first, second, in short," and the ending loves to rise into a moral.</div></div>
<div class="q">Voice: nowhere to breathe<button class="btn">Look</button><div class="ans">It reads straight through on one breath and never takes another. The feeling does not rise or fall, or it pauses where nobody would pause.</div></div>
</div>

<p>Still, do not lean on these too hard. The technology moves fast. A tell you can spot today may be gone tomorrow. A more reliable approach is to <strong>look at the source</strong>: who posted it, whether there is a citation, and whether anyone else has reported it.</p>

<div class="aside">Starting in September 2025, China put the Measures for Identifying AI-Generated Synthetic Content into effect. AI-generated content has to carry a label. So on the various platforms you will see small tags such as "AI-generated" or "suspected AI creation." When you post something AI made, remember to mark it the way the platform asks.</div>

## What an ordinary person can do with it

<ul>
<li>Make a birthday card just for your child, or pictures to go with a bedtime story.</li>
<li>If you run a small shop: a main product photo, a product description, a simple promo video.</li>
<li>When the writing will not start, let it draft something, then you revise.</li>
<li>Restore and colorize old photos from home.</li>
</ul>

<p>At the same time, watch out. Some people use an AI face swap or a copied voice to run a scam. If a "family member" calls on video, or sends a voice message, asking to borrow money, hang up and confirm another way, even when it looks and sounds exactly like them.</p>
